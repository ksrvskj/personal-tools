Introduction — the argument in outline

Thesis introduction · an outline for reading

This introduction does two things: it places the work within its field, and it relates its three studies. The three fall into two branches: two free the law of what a job brings, the third turns the arrival into a decision. Each a separate problem, and all from one classical root.

The narrative turns on one idea. Classical queueing theory takes the demand as given twice over: given in law (the distribution of what each job brings) and given in arrival (that the job comes at all is no one's decision). The modern work has loosened each of the two givens in turn; this thesis takes up both.

§1

The field

origins and the turn
Where the field begins · 

Queueing theory begins in the early twentieth century with Erlang's question: how many lines must a telephone exchange carry so that a caller rarely finds it engaged.Erlang

From that question grew a field: the stochastic analysis of demand placed on shared, and usually limited, resources, delivering waiting times, workloads, backlogs, utilisation.

A part moves through cutting, assembly and painting; a web request through a load balancer, an application server and a database; a video file is encoded, transmitted, stored — each a job carrying demands through the stages of a service network.

The stochastic analysis of such systems (begun by Erlang on telephone congestion at the start of the twentieth centuryErlang) delivers their waiting times, workloads, backlogs, utilisation.

The lasting classical lesson

Already in the M/G/1 queue the mean wait depends on the second moment of the service requirement: congestion follows the variability of what a job brings, not its mean alone.Pollaczek–Khinchine

One frame across domains

The same picture spans manufacturing, computing and media: a job as a carrier of demands moving through the stages of a network — the image the later chapters open on.

Data reshaped the models

Measurements of data-network traffic in the 1990s broke the classical assumptions about how demand arrives and what it carries.

Scale, asymptotics, universality

At the scale of modern systems, asymptotic analysis is a standard mode, not a concession.

Its limits reveal laws invisible in the microscopic detail and shared across systems that differ in that detail.Whitt

The absorbed features, and the turn

Modern models have taken in non-Markovian dynamics, heavy tails, dependence within and across a job's stages, time variation, heterogeneity, strategic behaviour.

Behind that variety lies one simplification the classical theory made and the modern work relaxes: it took the demand as given twice over; this thesis works where each given is loosened.

§2

The two directions

the classical model, and how it opened
The inheritance, and how the field split it

Given in law: what a job brings is a distribution handed to the model. Given in arrival: the stream may bend to the system's state, be blocked or lost, but its rule is handed, never chosen — on the demand's side, no one decides.Erlang

From this common trunk the field grew in several directions; the two this thesis walks loosen these two givens separately: directions of research, not a partition of systems.

Direction one: the law relaxed

Keep the arrival's rule handed and free the law of what a job brings: heavy tails, dependence within a job and across its stages, and rate–duration dependence: the transmission rate tied to the duration.

This line does explain its laws (by a source mechanism,Taqqu et al. or by fitting to measurementMaulik et al.) but every explanation ends in another law: the demand stays exogenous and autonomous, never responding to the congestion it creates.

Its deliverable is the law of congestion: exact where structure allows, asymptotic at scale.Kaj–Taqqu

Direction two: the arrival becomes a decision

Now whether the job comes is someone's decision: the designer's, the customer's, or both, since a toll is the designer moving the customer's calculus; with the customer's step, demand becomes endogenous: an equilibrium rather than an input.Lippman–Stidham

The tools change with it: fixed-point analysis beside distributional; individual-against-social and decentralised-against-centralised comparisons; instruments: prices, tolls, information, inventory targets.

The next two sections follow the two directions; the last returns to the thesis.

§3

What a job carries

exact and asymptotic analysis
An exact answer, where the model allows it

The strongest thing to ask of such a model is an exact answer: the full stationary law written in closed form. The Markovian queues give it: the M/M/1 queue is geometric.Kleinrock

Transforms reach further

Beyond the Markovian case, transform methods still pin the law down exactly, even where it has no closed form; and the infinite-server queue stays solvable whatever the service times: M/G/∞ keeps a Poisson count under any duration distribution, an insensitivity property.Takács; Cohen

Where exactness breaks — and where it does not

Exactness is fragile: general service laws, dependence among the characteristics of a single job, time variation, complex service disciplines all cost it.

But interaction between queues alone does not: Markovian networks with independent marks keep exact product-form laws. What breaks exactness in a network is what a job carries with it across the stages — the subject of this thesis.Jackson; Kelly

The reading problem

These exact characterisations arrive as transforms or integral equations: exact and versatile, and silent until they are inverted, expanded, or passed to a limit.

Approximation, and the systematic route

Numerical inversion, simulation and heuristics answer locally; asymptotic analysis is the systematic route (fluid limits, diffusion approximations, scaling limits), predictive already at moderate size.

Reading a system at scale

At the scale of modern systems, the way to read them is to rescale time and space and pass to a limit: the workload converges to a limiting process that captures the large-scale behaviour, and that process becomes the object of study.

The oldest such limit is heavy traffic: as utilisation approaches one the rescaled workload converges to reflected Brownian motion, and under heavy-tailed service to a reflected stable process — already a sign that the input law, not the regime alone, chooses the limit.Iglehart–WhittBoxma–Cohen

The data break

The measurements that forced the change: packet traffic self-similar and long-range dependent across time scales,Leland et al. file sizes and durations heavy-tailed over orders of magnitude,Crovella–Bestavros transmission rates highly variable and tied to size.Zhang et al.

A second way limits arise — from the data

The traffic data brought a second mechanism — and the setting this thesis works in: an infinite-source Poisson stream, its sessions arriving as a Poisson process and each transmitting for a heavy-tailed period, and the source population and the observation window grown together, so that nothing is pushed to the edge of stability as it is in heavy traffic.Taqqu et al.

Which limiting process appears is set by the regime and the joint law of a job's rate and duration together:D'Auria–Resnick when the heavy work is shared across many jobs the limit is Gaussian — fractional Brownian motion; when a single job carries it, a short such job gives a stable Lévy process and a window-spanning one a Telecom-type limit.Kaj–Taqqu; Gaigalas–Kaj

What this thesis drops

Two assumptions have stood across this whole line: that a job's rate is independent of its duration, and that there is a single resource.

This thesis drops both: it carries dependent job characteristics through a network, and asks what the mark (the rate and duration a job carries) does across the stages.Pipiras–Taqqu (2008)

§4

The arrival becomes a decision

strategic customers, and inventory
From handed rules to Naor's game

Impatience and admission entered first as handed rules (balking, renegingHaight) and as the controller's optimisation;Lippman–Stidham Naor's step put the customer's own utility behind the rule.Naor

In an observable M/M/1 a customer joins by weighing reward against expected waiting cost, and the equilibrium is a threshold.

Because a joiner ignores the delay it imposes on those who come later, the individually chosen threshold never falls below the socially optimal one — and a toll aligns them.

Unobservable queues, and the information axis

When the queue cannot be observed, admission is priced instead — and there, in the basic homogeneous model, the profit-maximising fee and the social optimum coincide.Edelson–Hildebrand

The field organised itself along this information axis, observable against unobservable; chapter 1's setting is the unobservable side.Hassin

The variants sweep out heterogeneous and multi-class customers and richer information structures,Mendelson until congestion information itself (its value, disclosure, price) becomes an object of study.

Choice beyond joining

The same logic reaches beyond joining to the choice of routes and providers (a tradition older than the queueing game) where self-interested routing settles at an equilibrium measurably worse than the system optimum.Wardrop

The systems here are not pure queues: they hold inventory

Inventory theory optimises stock against a demand it takes as given: from Harris's economic order quantity to base-stock, or order-up-to, policies.HarrisScarf

The shortfall of a base-stock system is itself a single-server queue, so a make-to-stock system is a queue whose inventory sets the waiting.Buzacott–Shanthikumar

With several demand classes on one line that stock is rationed across them; it is this inventory, shaping the wait, that a strategic customer reads when deciding whether to join.Ha; de Véricourt

Strategic customers meet inventory

Within make-to-stock systems, demand responsiveness has entered at the aggregate level: the revenue management of a make-to-stock queue.Caldentey–Wein

Closest to this thesis is a single-product make-to-stock queue with unobservable inventory and strategic joining, comparing decentralised and centralised objectives.Öz–Karaesmen

Its extensions stay single-product, varying the lever or the flow: setup costs, dynamic service, pricing and disclosure, retrials, two echelons.

We are not aware of prior work combining several strategic customer classes on one production line under unobservable inventory: the multi-class machinery exists without the game,Abouee-Mehrizi et al. the game without the classes.

§5

This thesis

the three studies
What the three share

This thesis takes up three studies along these lines. They were not cut from a single template; each was chosen as a problem worth solving in its own right. Set side by side, however, they share more than a common setting.

The more is this: for all their separateness, the three run along just two branches (two freeing the law of what a job brings, the third turning the arrival into a decision), the two sides of the one given twice over.

Chapter 1: strategic two-class make-to-stockCh. 1

Two customer classes arrive at a single production line and one shared first-come queue; the producer holds a base-stock target for each product, a joining customer triggers exactly one production job, and neither inventory nor backlog is observed.

A customer joins by weighing reward against price and anticipated waiting; the performance measures (waiting times, inventory levels, backlogs) are obtained in closed form.

Each class's incentive to join falls as either class joins more; a Nash equilibrium always exists, is unique whenever some target is positive, and degenerates to a continuum only on a knife-edge at zero inventory.

Above the customers sit two optimisations: a producer setting targets as a Stackelberg leader, with finite optimal levels, and a planner coordinating targets and participation through the balance of holding against waiting cost.

Producer and planner can part ways over whom to exclude: the coincidence of profit and welfare familiar from the simplest model gives way. And the step over the closest ancestor is the second class on a shared line.Öz–Karaesmen

a correction scheme (class-specific tolls or subsidies implementing the planner's participation as an equilibrium) recurs here, Naor's toll inside the inventory system.

Chapter 2: exact transforms with dependent marksCh. 2

A Poisson stream of jobs, each carrying a duration and a transmission rate at every stage, with dependence allowed arbitrarily within a job and across its stages.

Removing capacity constraints (immediate service) is what keeps the analysis exact while the demand is freed in full generality; the arrival itself stays Poisson.

The deliverables are explicit Laplace–Stieltjes transforms for the joint law of job counts and workloads, at an instant and cumulatively; tandem and parallel networks are solved, and the two motifs compose to any acyclic topology.

The model generalises the unit-rate M/G/∞ queue, drops the rate–duration independence standing in the scaling literature, and reads as the infinite-capacity limit of a loss network.

the steady-state start is handled exactly, through length-biased residual laws — and under heavy tails the initial population drains slowly enough to matter, the framework itself flagging what chapter 3 then scales.

The hinge

These transforms characterise the very model chapter 3 will scale — exact, yet silent in the sense §3 gave the word; the limit is their systematic reading, and chapter 3 takes it.

Chapter 3: the scaling limitsCh. 3

The model of chapter 2 on two nodes, with the arrival intensity growing together with the observation window; the object is the joint cumulative workload, at the level of its finite-dimensional distributions.

Three questions order the results: does the rescaled work average out; if not, is the heavy work shared among many jobs or carried by one; and if by one, how long is that job against the window.

When the work averages out the limit is a bivariate Wiener process, the same for either topology; when heavy work is shared it is Gaussian with fractional-Brownian marginals, and the network survives only in the dependence between the two nodes.

The two topologies part here: symmetric parallel service gives a genuine bivariate fractional Brownian motion, while the tandem's dependence is one-sided: a forward time shift, never bivariate fractional Brownian motion.

When one job dominates, its length selects the limit: a short job a stable Lévy process, a window-length job a Telecom-type limit, a spanning job a degenerate line.

The headline: the law of the marks selects the type of the limit; the network enters only through the dependence between nodes, and only when the work is heavy — a single family, rate tied to duration through a Gaussian copula, sweeping the picture out.

The thread

One thing recurs across all three: what one part of the demand does to the rest of the system. Naor was the first to put a price on it.

In chapter 1 a joining customer lengthens the wait for everyone behind: a congestion externality. In chapter 3 the two nodes' workloads move together only through the jobs that pass through both: the network's one trace in the limit. In chapter 2 that same cross-stage dependence is there from the outset, built into the model rather than emerging from it.

Scope, and order

The reach is deliberately bounded. The scaling limits are for two nodes; the exact transforms, for any acyclic network of tandem and parallel pieces. General topologies and feedback routing are left for later, the convergence is taken at the level of the finite-dimensional distributions with tightness not yet shown, and first-come service is kept as chapter 1's baseline.

The order of the chapters runs one way: closed-form performance measures, then exact transforms, then limit laws, giving up explicitness for generality at each step.
